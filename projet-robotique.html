ROBOT

Robotics · Autonomous inspection

Patrol, detect, report.

An autonomous inspection robot, entirely in simulation: it patrols a building, spots cracks and stains on the walls, places them on the map and writes its own PDF report.

The problem

— Key question: can a robot patrol on its own, spot the defects, place them on a map and write the report?

Inspecting walls by hand is slow, costly and subjective: two inspectors do not always see the same defects.

The system

Six steps, from patrol to report.

  • Step — 01

    Simulated world

    A three-zone building in Gazebo, with 8 known defects: 4 cracks and 4 stains.

    Gazebo
  • Step — 02

    Patrol

    Nav2 drives the TurtleBot3 robot through 11 points.

    Nav2TurtleBot3
  • Step — 03

    Camera

    640 × 480 images at 2 Hz, without a depth camera.

  • Step — 04

    Detection

    YOLOv8n spots cracks and stains, confidence threshold 0.4.

    YOLOv8 (Ultralytics)
  • Step — 05

    Localisation

    A ray cast on the SLAM map gives the position of the defect.

    SLAM Toolbox
  • Step — 06

    Report

    A PDF is written automatically at the end of the patrol.

    See a sample report (PDF in French, opens in a viewer)

The approach

From the simulated wall to the final report.

  1. 01

    A simulated world

    Defect positions stored as ground truth. Floor and lighting tuned first: the robot was slipping and the image was white.

  2. 02

    A dataset

    466 images taken by teleporting the robot, 19% of them without any defect. Split by position, not by image, plus a test_unseen set with 8 new defects.

  3. 03

    The detector

    YOLOv8n trained on Google Colab for 60 epochs. YOLOv8s gives the same accuracy but is 2.4 times slower: YOLOv8n is kept.

  4. 04

    Navigation

    Map built with SLAM Toolbox, then a Nav2 patrol. A missed point is retried once, then reported.

  5. 05

    Localisation

    The direction of the defect in the image becomes a ray on the map. The first wall it hits gives the position.

  6. 06

    Severity, tracking, report

    The height of the defect is estimated to give a severity level. Two patrols are compared: new, gone, worse. The PDF report is generated.

Results

Measurements, in simulation.

  • 0.995

    mAP@0.5 on the test set

  • 11.8 cm

    median position error

  • 4 / 8

    complete patrols

  • 0.988

    mAP@0.5 on never-seen defects

  • 6.3 cm

    gap between patrols

  • 7.3%

    median error on the estimated height

All scores are measured in simulation: they say nothing about real walls.

Limitations

What does not work yet.

  • 01

    Navigation

    Nav2 completes 4 patrols out of 8. The cause of the planner's “empty plan” has not been established.

  • 02

    Strong lighting

    No complete patrol out of 4 in strong light. The detector alone stays close to 1.

  • 03

    Localisation

    Once, AMCL was off by about 3 m, which produced 12 false positives.

  • 04

    Simulation only

    No test on real walls.

We document them because a good detector is not enough: the robot also has to reach the wall.

What we learned

A good detector is not enough.

The hardest part was not the model, but integrating it into a system that navigates, localises correctly and turns observations into usable information.

  • Document failures as much as successes
  • Measure on never-seen images (test_unseen)
  • Count demo runs separately from the evaluation
  • Everything is evaluated in simulation, not yet in a real environment
